The system

Overlap makes the cells

Discs are scattered until they form one connected silhouette. Every region you can see exists because two discs met — a cell has no boundary of its own, only the edges it inherits. Nothing is drawn over the top. The structure is a consequence of relation, not a decoration applied to it.

Cut, not printed

Every pattern is stroke and void. The page shows through all of it, so the mark behaves like pierced metal rather than ink — it changes with whatever sits behind it, and it never reads as a solid logo blocking the ground.

One hot cell

Six chalky colours per mark and exactly one saturated. It lands wherever the seed puts it. Without that single hot cell the whole thing goes polite; with two, it goes loud.

Everyone gets a different one

Each mark comes from a six‑character seed. No visitor gets the same pattern twice; every visitor gets the same family. Share the seed and you share the mark — akinn.com/?seed= .

Where the tilings come from

Ten repeats, each constructed from scratch out of ornament that is centuries old and in the public domain — and each of which arose independently in several traditions at once. That independent recurrence is the whole argument: these forms are already kin before anyone arranges them.

On the reference. The concept — ornament from traditions and centuries that could never have met, brought into one form until you notice they are related — is Nevin Aladağ's, from her Pattern Kinship series, seen at the Harn Museum of Art. Credit it publicly if this ships.

On what is ours. Nothing is traced or sampled. The tilings are built from public-domain historical ornament — the same well she drew from, not her results. The partition rule is different in kind: her cells are divided by drawn curves that sweep across the composition independently of the discs beneath them, which is her signature gesture and is deliberately absent here. Ours are produced only by disc overlap, computed rather than composed. The palette logic, the geometry and the interaction are ours.

Before this ships. Have an IP lawyer look at it — concept adoption is defensible, but a real identity deserves a real sign-off. And consider the other route: commissioning or licensing from the artist is available, more respectful, and often less expensive than the legal comfort of going it alone.
