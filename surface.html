The three dialects

Shown loud so you can see them. Every one is built on the same 40 px lattice as the base circle net — identical skeleton, different connective marks. All public domain, all recurring independently across several traditions.

What changed, and why

Kept: the volume

Your contrast was right and I did not touch it. A surface that competes with content is a failed surface, and the earlier marks I built were all logo-loud. This stays at a whisper.

Added: something to be akin to

One motif cannot express kinship — there is nothing for it to be related to. Three dialects on one shared lattice can. The skeleton is the family; the marks are the difference.

Fixed: the blobs

They were the only element with no rule behind them, which is why they read as a blur layer and went muddy in dark. Now they are zone boundaries. Same shape, same softness, but they finally do something.

The asset

One tile, 480 px, seamless in both axes. Nested patterns keep it small — the base net and each dialect are 40 px sub-tiles, so the whole thing is well under 2 KB before compression.

Tile480 × 480 — twelve lattice steps, so discs never land in the same relation twice within a screen
Lattice40 px pitch, circle radius 28.3 — every dialect shares it exactly
ColourcurrentColor throughout. Inline the SVG and it inherits your text colour, so one asset serves both themes and any brand recolour.
Opacitynet .09, disc tint .04, dialect .10, nodes .20. Drop each by about a fifth for dark.
WeightVector. No retina asset, no @2x, no dark-mode duplicate, no tinting workaround.
