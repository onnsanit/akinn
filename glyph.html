Why this glyph is the right seed

Drawn large, the argument is visible. This is a better foundation than either mark I built earlier, and I would rebuild the identity from it.

Two forms, not one
The same open rounded square, twice, turned and offset. Congruent but never aligned — alike, not identical. One motif can only be decorative; two can be related.
Different voices
Each form takes its own colour off one restrained wheel. Slate and sage are neighbours, not opposites.
The join is marked
The bone bar sits exactly where the two forms cross. It is the only light value in the system, and it appears nowhere else — so the brightest thing in the brand is always the point of contact.
Open ends
Every line overshoots its corner and simply stops. Nothing is a closed container, so nothing encloses or excludes. That is also what stops it reading as a logo in a box.

What I would do next

Keep both tiles

Surface for anything behind content. Signature for splash, empty states, share cards and marketing. One glyph, two volumes — which is the system I said was missing last time.

Rebuild the mark from this

A single glyph at large scale, cropped close, is already an excellent logo: two related forms and a marked join. Far better than the disc clusters. The earlier work becomes reference, not foundation.

Watch the accent

The bone bar is the one element that can wreck the surface. Rule: full strength in the signature and the mark, dimmed to its neighbours everywhere content sits on top. Never two bright joins in one viewport.

Numbers

Signature tile560 × 560, glyph at 100 %, four placements, each rotated differently. Seamless both axes.
Surface tile246 × 246, glyph at 44 %, same four rotations. Seamless both axes.
Line weight2.2 signature, 1.5 surface — scaled by less than the glyph, so thin lines survive the reduction.
ContrastSignature at full colour. Surface at .22 opacity, accent at .30 instead of 1.
PaletteGround #10263F · slate #7E9CBB · sage #93A98D · bone #E4E2D8. Light theme: paper #F1F0EA, lines darkened to #4A6684 and #5E7358.

Every placement is drawn nine times so glyphs that run off one edge return on the other — both tiles repeat cleanly in both axes with no visible seam. All geometry is vector and recolours from four variables, so neither tile needs a raster export, a retina variant or a dark-mode duplicate.
