What changed

Left is the behaviour in your original, right is the revision. Same geometry, same palette family.

The palette, used as designed

Your system already carries hierarchy in its tiers — hue is the asset class, shade is risk, darker is riskier. The original spent every circle at full strength, which throws that away and makes six loud hues compete. Here the bulk sits in tiers 1–2 and the darker tiers are reserved for positions that genuinely are riskier, so the picture is calmer and more truthful at the same time.

Notes for the build

Size encodingCircle area is proportional to value, never diameter. Diameter-scaling overstates large holdings by roughly the square — a real misreading risk in a wealth chart. The key on the plate gives the reader a reference.
Canopy packingLargest holding sits on the stem axis; the rest attach outward with a fixed 6 % overlap, so canopies look organic but never pile into unreadable knots.
SeparationEvery circle carries a 2 px stroke in the plate colour. Overlaps then read as one disc in front of another rather than as a new colour that means nothing.
The forkDerived from canopy radius rather than fixed, at ±32°, with the canopy resting on the apex. It now reads as carrying the mass.
The houseFilled in Real Estate tier 4 #522A7C, which is what your own rules say a single illiquid property is. It stops being an outline outlier and joins the system.
Dark themeEvery tier lifts one step lighter so tier 4 stays visible on a dark plate. Hue still identifies the class and the relative ordering is preserved, so nothing about the encoding breaks.

The wordmark sits below, centred, at roughly a third of the tallest canopy, with the descriptor locked underneath at a fixed tracking. Keep the gap between baseline and wordmark equal to one stem height — it is the only spacing rule this lockup needs.

Units are placeholders. Swap the key and the axis for your own currency and periods. Values here are invented to exercise the layout across a plausible range.
